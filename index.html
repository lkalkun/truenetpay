Guide & Methodology

How UK Take-Home Pay & Employer Costs Are Calculated (2026/27)

Understanding the difference between your gross salary, net take-home pay, and the true cost to an employer helps both employees planning personal budgets and businesses forecasting payroll commitments.

1. Personal Allowance & Income Tax

Most UK taxpayers receive a standard tax-free Personal Allowance of £12,570 (tax code 1257L). In England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, earnings above this are taxed at 20% (Basic rate up to £50,270), 40% (Higher rate up to £125,140), and 45% (Additional rate). Scotland applies separate 6-band rates starting at 19%. For incomes over £100,000, the Personal Allowance tapers by £1 for every £2 earned.

2. Employee National Insurance (Class 1)

Employees pay Class 1 National Insurance at 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 per year (£242 to £967 per week), and 2% on all earnings above £50,270. Employees over State Pension age are exempt from paying Class 1 NI.

3. Employer National Insurance (15% Rate)

In addition to gross salary, employers pay Secondary Class 1 National Insurance at 15% on employee earnings above the £5,000 annual Secondary Threshold (£96/week). Reliefs apply for apprentices under 25, employees under 21, and armed forces veterans up to £50,270, while eligible businesses can claim the £10,500 Employment Allowance.

4. Workplace Pensions & Student Loans

Under UK auto-enrolment rules, minimum contributions are typically 5% from the employee and 3% from the employer on Qualifying Earnings (£6,240 to £50,270) or Total Gross Salary. Employee contributions reduce taxable income before tax is calculated. Student loans are deducted at 9% above the plan threshold (or 6% for Postgraduate loans).

5. Salary Sacrifice & £30,000 Worked Example

Salary sacrifice is an agreement where you give up part of your contractual gross salary in exchange for a non-cash benefit (such as workplace pension contributions, an EV car scheme, or Cycle to Work). Because your contractual pay is lower before tax and National Insurance are calculated, you save both Income Tax and Employee NI (plus Student Loan repayments), while your employer saves 15% Employer NI.

Effect on a £30,000 salary: At £30,000/yr, you pay 20% Basic Rate tax and 8% Employee NI (28% combined). If you sacrifice £1,500/yr (5%) into a pension instead of a standard relief-at-source scheme, you save an extra £120/yr in Employee NI (8% × £1,500)—meaning £1,500 goes into your pension pot while reducing your take-home pay by only £1,080/yr (£90/month), and saving your employer £225/yr in Employer NI.

6. How Bonus Payments Are Taxed

Annual or one-off bonuses are taxed through PAYE on top of your regular salary at your marginal rate—not a separate "bonus tax." For example, if your regular salary is £45,000 and you receive a £5,000 bonus, it falls within the Basic Rate band (20% Income Tax + 8% Employee NI = 28% deductions, so you keep 72%).

However, if a bonus pushes your total annual income above £50,270, the slice above £50,270 is taxed at 40% Income Tax + 2% NI (42% combined). If a bonus pushes your income between £100,000 and £125,140, the £1-for-£2 Personal Allowance taper creates an effective marginal tax and NI rate of 62% (or 69.5% in Scotland).

7. How the Reverse Salary Lookup Is Calculated

When negotiating a job offer or budgeting around a fixed monthly take-home goal, you often know the net pay you need rather than the gross salary to ask for. Because UK PAYE deductions step up across multiple thresholds—such as the £12,570 Personal Allowance, the £50,270 Higher Rate boundary, student loan thresholds, and the £100,000–£125,140 Personal Allowance taper—there is no single formula that reverses net pay in one step.

Our Reverse Salary Lookup uses an iterative numerical solver (50-step binary search) over the full HMRC PAYE engine using your currently selected tax year, region, tax code, pension percentage, salary sacrifice, and student loan plan. It tests candidate gross salaries until the resulting take-home pay matches your target to within a fraction of a penny, and also shows the total Employer Cost / Yr required to fund that net salary.
